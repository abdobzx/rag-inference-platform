Active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) must be stored at the temperature specified on their certificate of analysis, typically between 15C and 25C for solid dosage form excipients.
Cold-chain ingredients such as certain biologics and enzymes require storage between 2C and 8C and must never be exposed to ambient temperature for more than 30 minutes during transfer.
Every ingredient batch must be logged with its lot number, supplier certificate of analysis reference, and receipt date before it can be released for use in production.
Cross-contamination prevention requires that allergenic ingredients such as lactose, gluten-derived excipients, and certain preservatives be handled in a dedicated, physically separated preparation zone.
Before weighing any active ingredient, operators must verify the batch has not exceeded its retest date and that the container seal is intact.
Weighing and dispensing of active ingredients must be performed under a calibrated balance with daily calibration checks logged in the equipment logbook.
Any deviation greater than 0.5% from the target weight during dispensing must be documented as a quality event and requires supervisor sign-off before the batch proceeds.
Water used in ingredient preparation must meet Purified Water USP or Ph. Eur. specifications, with conductivity and microbial testing performed per batch.
Equipment used for ingredient mixing must be cleaned and the cleaning validated between different product runs to prevent cross-contamination, following the site's cleaning validation protocol.
Personnel handling active pharmaceutical ingredients must wear appropriate PPE including gloves, mask, and where required, a full cleanroom gown, per the ingredient's material safety data sheet.
Excipients with a known incompatibility (for example, certain amine-based excipients with reducing sugars) must not be pre-mixed and stored together prior to formulation.
Every ingredient preparation step must be recorded in the batch manufacturing record in real time, not reconstructed afterward, to remain compliant with good manufacturing practice (GMP) documentation rules.
If an ingredient's certificate of analysis shows an out-of-specification result for assay purity, the batch must be quarantined and cannot be used until quality assurance releases it.
Humidity-sensitive ingredients such as certain hygroscopic powders must be prepared in a controlled environment below 40% relative humidity to prevent caking or degradation.
The maximum allowable holding time between ingredient dispensing and use in the next manufacturing step is defined per product in the site's validated process parameters, and exceeding it requires re-testing.
Ingredient preparation anomalies - such as unexpected color change, unusual odor, or a discrepancy between expected and actual yield - must be reported immediately to the quality assurance team, not resolved informally by the operator.
Light-sensitive active ingredients, including several vitamin and hormone compounds, must be prepared and stored in amber glass or opaque containers to prevent photodegradation.
Reconciliation of ingredient quantities (amount received, amount used, amount remaining, amount wasted) is mandatory at the end of every preparation batch and must balance within the site's approved tolerance.
